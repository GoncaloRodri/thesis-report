Anonymity Networks such as Tor have emerged as essential tools for safeguarding end-to-end online communication and combating censorship, serving millions of users worldwide. However, recent research has demonstrated that powerful state-level adversaries can potentially perform de-anonymization attacks using traffic correlation and web interaction fingerprinting techniques based on advanced machine and deep learning correlation models. This dissertation addresses these challenges by enhancing the Tor network's defenses against the aforementioned attacks. The primary objective is to propose a solution that improves the functionality of Tor relay nodes by leveraging the existing software architecture to support differentially private circuits—a mechanism for end-to-end enhanced privacy-preserving communication inspired by the principles of differential privacy. The proposed approach aims to achieve practical levels of throughput and latency suitable for real-world application scenarios, while providing users with adaptable and more resilient anonymity protection. 

At the core of the proposed design is the integration of carefully bounded random noise into traffic flows established within Tor circuits. This noise is introduced through a combination of randomized jitter and Tor-based fake cells within the onion-routing structure among relay nodes, reducing temporal and packet-size correlations that could be exploited by advanced machine learning-based adversaries. The overarching goal is to mitigate advanced traffic correlation and fingerprinting attacks, thereby enhancing the privacy guarantees of the Tor network. 

To the best of our knowledge, the contributions of this dissertation represent a potentially significant step toward incorporating differential privacy into the software architecture of Tor relay nodes. Furthermore, the proposed differential privacy-inspired approach provides a formally grounded, scalable, and effective mechanism for enhancing online anonymity, offering increased resilience against state-of-the-art Internet censorship and surveillance techniques.
